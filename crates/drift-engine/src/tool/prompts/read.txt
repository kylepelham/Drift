Reads a file from the filesystem and returns its contents with line numbers (`N: text`). Overwriting a file with `write`, or deleting it, is refused until this session has read it; `edit` copies its text from what `read` shows.

- Returns up to 2000 lines starting at `offset`; the tail of the output says how to read the rest. A negative `offset` reads the last that many lines, as for the end of a log.
- Long lines are cut at 2000 characters.
- Given a directory it lists the entries instead.
- Images (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, up to 32 MB, scaled to 2000 pixels and 5 MB) and PDFs (up to 10 MB) come back for you to look at. A PDF over 10 pages is read in parts with `pages` ("1-5", at most 20 pages a read), which also reads a part of one up to 100 MB. Other binary files are refused. A text file over 10 MB (a log, generated output) is read a page at a time: give `offset` to go deeper; it does not say how many lines it has.
- Prefer one larger read to many small slices; to find a passage in a large file, `grep` for it.
- Several reads run at once when you issue them together in one reply.
